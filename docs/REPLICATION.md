## 1. How to read this

- **Daily claims:** event-sample local projections, the pre-registered primary daily specification (protocol §9). Each outcome's change runs from the last observation before the announcement to the h-th observation after it, using that series' own observation time. Controls are the five pre-event daily changes, plus the other JK shock where relevant. Standard errors are HC3, and bands are 90%.
- **Monthly claims:** local projections on monthly data, with 12 lags of the outcome and the shock and Newey–West errors (h+1 lags). The Känzig news shock has standard deviation 0.59.
- **Units:** yields in bp; prices, FX and indices in % (100 × log change). Fed surprises are scaled per 10 bp; OPEC surprises per 1% move in oil futures.
- **Verdict:**
  - *replicated*: the sign matches and the 90% band excludes zero.
  - *same sign*: the sign matches but the band includes zero.
  - *opposite*: the sign is wrong.
- **Rows marked *(mechanical)*** are close to true by construction. For example, the 30-minute Fed surprise sits inside the daily window. They check the plumbing, not the economics.
- **Rows ending in `r`** are reference runs for comparisons (Bauer–Swanson raw vs orthogonalized).

**Summary:** of 15 claims (excluding reference rows), 13 replicated, 2 have the expected sign without significance, 0 have the opposite sign. Among the 9 non-mechanical claims, 8 replicated.

## 5. Timing check: when do Canadian bond yields close? (open item O-1)

The BoC does not document the time of its closing yields. BoC announcements are in the morning, so this only matters for 14:00 ET FOMC announcements. If the close is after 14:00, the FOMC day is day 0; if before, the next day is. The response of Canadian yields to a 10 bp Fed policy (MP) shock under both assumptions:

| outcome | assumed close (ET) | day 0 | day 1 | N |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CA2Y | 16:00 | +5.63 [+4.14, +7.12] | +6.42 [+4.15, +8.69] | 221 |
| CA2Y | 13:30 | +2.14 [−0.41, +4.70] | +1.33 [−2.01, +4.67] | 221 |
| CA10Y | 16:00 | +3.03 [+1.29, +4.76] | +2.88 [−0.05, +5.80] | 221 |
| CA10Y | 13:30 | +1.29 [−0.20, +2.77] | +0.71 [−1.36, +2.79] | 221 |

Two hypotheses make opposite predictions. If the close is after 14:00, the 16:00-assumed day-0 window contains the announcement and should respond strongly, while the 13:30-assumed day 0 (the next day) holds only later news. If the close is before 14:00, the reverse holds.

**Conclusion: the data indicate the Valet closing yields are taken after 14:00 ET.** The 2-year response is significant in the 16:00-assumed window and its estimate lies above the 13:30-assumed band. 16:00 stays the working assumption, and open item O-1 is resolved empirically.

## 6. Notes on disagreements and caveats

- **JK1-JK4, BS1, KZ1.** Marked mechanical: the surprise is measured inside the daily window (or, for KZ1, in oil futures that move with spot WTI). They confirm the plumbing (timestamps, windows, units) rather than test economics.
- **JK4.** The information shock lifts stocks by construction in the 30-minute window, but the effect is not significant over the whole day, while its effect on rates (JK3) is strong. The equity part of information effects looks short-lived or too noisy to see at daily frequency. This is reported, not explained away.
- **BS3 vs BS3r.** With the raw surprise, industrial production rises after a tightening: the classic puzzle. With the orthogonalized surprise it falls, significantly. Bauer and Swanson attribute the puzzle to the Fed responding to news markets had not yet priced; removing the predictable part of the surprise fixes it. This reproduces the paper's central point.
- **KZ5.** Activity falls, as the paper reports, but the monthly LP is imprecise at month 12 and only significant at the longest horizon (horizon table). The paper uses a VAR, which is more efficient at long horizons than an LP; M4 compares the two directly.
- **KZ3.** The paper's dollar result comes from a monthly VAR; our test asks a narrower daily question (does the dollar weaken in the week after OPEC news). The sign agrees and the effect is small.
- **FX1, FX2.** Expected signs taken from the project brief, not verified paper claims; they are included as stylized facts.
- **BoC proxy.** For the 10-year yield and equities, the event LP and the Rigobon-Sack estimate differ. The event LP assumes the proxy contains only BoC news; Rigobon-Sack does not. Other same-day news that moves Canadian yields and stocks together would bias the event LP for those outcomes, so Rigobon-Sack is the more credible estimate there. M4 reports both for every Canadian outcome.
