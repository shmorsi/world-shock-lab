## 1. Two estimators of the same thing: proxy BVAR vs LP-IV (monthly)

Response to a US monetary policy shock that raises the 1-year yield by 25 bp on impact. Units: yields in percentage points, other variables in % (100 × log). Both estimators use the same variables, instrument and lags. The BVAR imposes VAR dynamics and Minnesota shrinkage (lower variance, possible bias); the LP-IV does not (less bias, more variance). docs/SIMULATION.md shows the BVAR's bootstrap bands under-cover in simulation, so where the two disagree, read the LP bands as the honest measure of uncertainty.

## 7. Notes

- **Brief's state-dependence goal.** Not met. None of the 80 pre-registered regime tests survives the false-discovery-rate control, and the raw count of small p-values is only modestly above what chance alone would produce. The minimum detectable effects show why: with these event counts only large regime differences are visible. The most suggestive cells before correction (the Fed's effect on the US 2-year at one week being smaller when VIX is high; OPEC news moving the TSX less when inflation is high) are hypotheses for the live data to test, not findings. No further specifications were searched.
- **Canada lens.** A Fed move and a BoC move of the same size in their own 2-year yields move the Canadian 10-year yield by similar amounts, and the Canadian dollar by similar amounts in opposite directions. Canadian equities are where they differ: the Fed's effect on the TSX, the Big-6 banks and energy is clearly negative and robust, while the BoC's is small in the event LP. The Rigobon-Sack estimates narrow that gap, so the size of the Fed's advantage depends on the estimator; its direction does not.
- **BoC proxy contamination.** The network finds the BoC proxy 'moving' US yields. A BoC decision should barely move US Treasuries, so this is evidence that the daily proxy still carries common North American rate news despite passing its M3 validation. It is the reason Rigobon-Sack estimates are shown next to every BoC result, and why BoC equity results are classed as fragile.
- **VAR vs LP.** Monthly identification is weak for both instruments, so these are the least reliable results in the project. The BVAR's prior dominates (many coefficients per equation on a short sample) and pulls most responses toward zero with narrow bands; simulation shows those bands are too narrow. The LP-IV with the orthogonalized Bauer-Swanson surprise shows the textbook contraction, and for US industrial production at one year and US and Canadian prices at two years even the weak-instrument-robust Anderson-Rubin sets, though very wide, exclude zero. With the JK surprise the signs are puzzling and nothing is significant. Daily event studies, where identification is strong, carry the project's main causal claims.
- **Oil exporters vs importers.** Every country ETF rises on negative oil supply news, and the ordering matches oil exposure (Norway, Canada and Australia most; India least), but the exporter-importer difference is not significant. The ETFs are priced in US dollars and OPEC news may coincide with global demand news, so the common rise is not by itself evidence about exporters.
