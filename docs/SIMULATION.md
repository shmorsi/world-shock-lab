## Data-generating process

Structural VAR(2) with three variables (0 = policy rate, 1 = activity, 2 = prices), Y_t = A1 Y_{t-1} + A2 Y_{t-2} + B ε_t, with companion spectral radius 0.879. The responses estimated are those of variable 1 (and 0 and 2 where shown) to shock 0, for h = 0..12.

```
A1 =
    [  0.70,   0.10,   0.05]
    [ -0.15,   0.60,   0.05]
    [  0.05,   0.10,   0.70]
A2 =
    [  0.15,   0.00,   0.00]
    [ -0.05,   0.15,   0.00]
    [  0.00,   0.05,   0.15]
B =
    [  0.50,   0.00,   0.00]
    [ -0.10,   0.80,   0.00]
    [ -0.05,   0.20,   0.40]
```

Pass criteria (M2, docs/PLAN.md §6): |bias| ≤ 5% of the peak response + 4 Monte Carlo s.e. at every horizon; mean coverage of the 90% band in [0.85, 0.95], with no horizon below 0.80.

## What this shows

- **LP with dense shocks:** Newey–West (h+1 lags) and lag-augmented HC errors both give 90% bands with mean coverage between 0.87 and 0.90, slightly below nominal, which is typical of finite samples. Relative bias is largest for the prices outcome only because its true peak response is small; in absolute terms it stays within a few Monte Carlo s.e.
- **Daily-like sparse, fat-tailed shocks:** the event-sample LP with HC3 errors, which is the pre-registered primary daily specification (protocol §9), covers 0.902. Time-series LPs with zero-filled shocks are unbiased but their bands are too narrow (0.862 with Newey–West). HC3 narrows the gap (0.875), which points to high-leverage event days. This is why zero-filled daily LPs are robustness checks only.
- **LP-IV, strong instrument** (median effective F 499): Wald bands cover 0.886.
- **LP-IV, weak instrument** (median effective F 1.14): the point estimate is meaningless (mean bias 65 times the peak response). Wald bands cover 0.993 here only because they are enormous, so they carry no information. Anderson–Rubin sets keep coverage 0.893. Every LP-IV result we report will therefore show the effective F and the Anderson–Rubin set.
- **Placebo:** an unrelated series produces responses indistinguishable from zero; the 90% band contains zero in 0.896 of draws.
- **Proxy BVAR:** percentile bootstrap bands are too narrow even without a prior (coverage 0.87 for a nominal 0.90), as Kilian (1998) documents for persistent VARs. Shrinkage then biases the normalized variable's own path toward zero (coverage 0.75). The responses we compare with LPs cover 0.84. This is the bias-variance trade-off: VAR bands are narrower than LP bands partly because they are too narrow, so wherever the two disagree the LP is the more trustworthy guide to uncertainty.
